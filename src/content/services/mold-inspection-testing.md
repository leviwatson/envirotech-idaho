intro:
  heading: Two jobs, two companies
  paragraphs:
    - "An inspection and a test are different things, and in our case they are done by different companies. That is worth understanding before you spend money on either."
    - "The inspection is ours and it is free. Someone comes out, looks at what you have, reads moisture through the surrounding material, works back toward the water source, and writes an estimate for any work that is needed. No samples are taken and nothing is charged."
    - "Testing is somebody else's job. If material or air needs analysing, you engage an independent testing company, they collect the samples, their laboratory processes them, and they issue the result to you. You pay them directly. We do not perform that work, we do not sell it, and we take no percentage of it."
    - "You will see companies advertise testing as though it came bundled into a no-cost visit. Some of them do both halves — they test, and then they quote to fix what their own test found. That arrangement is legal and it is common, and it is precisely the arrangement we do not have."

problem:
  heading: Why the separation is the whole point
  paragraphs:
    - "A company that both tests and remediates has an obvious interest in what the test says. If it finds something, the same company gets the removal work. Nobody involved has to be dishonest for that to shape outcomes; it is simply a bad structure to put a decision inside."
    - "We are the company that gets paid to remove mold. So we are the last people who should be deciding whether you have mold, and the last people who should be certifying that we successfully removed it. Both of those judgements belong somewhere else, and on our jobs they are."
    - "There is a practical consequence, and it is the most useful thing on this page. Because we do not sell testing, we have nothing to gain from telling you to buy it. When we look at a crawlspace and say a lab result will not change what you should do next, that advice costs us nothing to give. A company that bills for sampling cannot say that sentence as cheaply."
    - "The other failure worth naming is the hardware store kit. A settle plate left open on a counter will grow something in almost any building in Idaho, because ordinary indoor air contains mold spores. Without a control sample taken outdoors at the same time, and a laboratory reading both, a positive result tells you very little — and people make expensive decisions on the strength of it."

  heading: How testing actually runs
  intro: "Steps one and two are ours and they are free. From step three onward you are dealing with the testing company, not with us."
  steps:
    - title: Free inspection
      description: "Someone comes out and looks. We go where the problem is, including the crawlspace and the attic if that is where it lives, and we look at everywhere the moisture reached rather than only the spot you noticed."
    - title: A straight answer on whether to test
      description: "You get an honest read on whether a laboratory result would tell you something the inspection has not already established. If growth is visible and the source is obvious and nobody is in dispute, we will say testing is optional. If a result is needed for a sale, an insurer, or a tenancy, we will say that too."
    - title: You engage a testing company
      description: "If you decide to proceed, you contract an independent testing company directly and pay them directly. We are not in that transaction. If you want names of firms that operate in the Treasure Valley, ask and we will point you at some — with no arrangement behind it and nothing coming back to us."
    - title: Sample collection
      description: "Their technician collects what the situation calls for. Bulk or surface samples take a physical piece of material or a tape lift from the surface. Air sampling runs cassettes indoors alongside a control sample taken outdoors at the same time, because indoor air is compared against outdoor air rather than against zero."
    - title: Chain of custody
      description: "Samples are bagged, labeled, and logged against a form recording who handled them and when. That document is what makes a result usable by an insurer, a buyer's agent, or a landlord, rather than merely interesting."
    - title: Laboratory analysis
      description: "The samples are analyzed and the testing company issues the report. It goes to you, because you commissioned it. We never see it unless you choose to send it to us."
    - title: You decide what happens next
      description: "A result is a decision tool. Bring it to us and we will write a scope and a number against it, and you are free to bring it to anyone else instead. The report is yours and it does not commit you to us."

  - question: Do I actually need a lab test?
    answer: "Often not. If growth is visible, the moisture source is identified, and nobody is in dispute about it, a result mostly confirms what an inspection already established. Testing earns its cost when somebody else has to be convinced — a buyer, an insurer, a lender, a landlord, or a tenant — or when you want a documented baseline before and after work."
  - question: Are the home test kits any good?
    answer: "They are unreliable in the way that matters. A settle plate will grow something in almost any building, because ordinary indoor air contains spores. Without a simultaneous outdoor control sample and a laboratory reading both, a positive tells you very little, and people spend real money reacting to it."
