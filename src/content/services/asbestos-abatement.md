  paragraphs:

On our jobs that is an independent accredited laboratory, and it is not us. We do not collect our own clearance samples, we do not process them, and we do not get to interpret them in our favour."
    - "Most abatement firms self-clear. The same company that quoted the job, did the removal, and wants to be paid also signs the paper saying the air is clean. That is legal and it is common and it is a conflict of interest sitting at the exact point where you have no way to check the work yourself."
    - "Everything else on this page is standard practice done properly. Containment, negative pressure, air monitoring, disposal to a licensed facility. It matters, and we will explain all of it. But the reason to call us instead of the next company is the paragraph above."
    - "Abatement itself means one of three things depending on what you have: removing the material, encapsulating it so it cannot release fibres, or leaving intact material alone and documenting it. A good inspection tells you which of those you actually need, and sometimes the honest answer is the third one. Older houses that have taken water often have both problems at once, and [mold remediation](/mold-remediation/) runs the same containment-first way."

problem:
  heading: Why disturbing it is worse than leaving it
  paragraphs:
    - "Asbestos-containing material that is intact, undamaged, and not being disturbed is generally stable. The material is only a problem when fibres get into the air, and that happens when someone cuts it, sands it, scrapes it, breaks it, or demolishes around it. A popcorn ceiling nobody touches behaves differently from a popcorn ceiling somebody scraped on a Saturday."
    - "That is why the most common way a Treasure Valley homeowner creates an asbestos problem is by starting a renovation. Pulling up 9x9 floor tile and the black mastic under it. Scraping a textured ceiling. Cutting into duct wrap or pipe insulation in a crawlspace. Opening a wall in a pre-1980 house without knowing what is in it."
    - "Asbestos is a regulated material, and how it is handled, transported, and disposed of is governed rather than left to preference. Depending on the scope and the property, notification requirements can apply before work starts. Part of what you are paying for is somebody who knows which of those apply to your job and handles them."
    - "If you are planning a remodel rather than reacting to one, this is your decision point: test before demolition, not after. A few bulk samples and a lab result add two to five days to your schedule and cost a fraction of an abatement. What they buy you is not halting a job halfway to contain a space your crew has already worked in. [Testing before a remodel](/asbestos-testing-before-remodel/) covers the sequence."

process:
  heading: How an asbestos abatement job runs
  intro: "Seven steps, in this order. Air monitoring runs through the middle of it and an independent lab closes it out."
  steps:
    - title: Inspection and testing
      description: "Someone comes out and identifies what is suspect. Bulk samples go to an accredited laboratory to confirm whether the material actually contains asbestos and at what concentration. Plenty of jobs stop here, because the material comes back negative and there is nothing to abate. The inspection is free; the lab work is a separate paid service and you hear the cost first."
    - title: Containment
      description: "Poly sheeting seals the work area and every seam is taped. A negative air machine runs two pre-filters into a HEPA final stage and ducts to an exterior opening. A decontamination chamber goes in at the entry, so nobody walks out of the work area without passing through it."
    - title: Removal
      description: "The material comes out wet wherever wetting is appropriate, because keeping it damp keeps fibres down. It is double-bagged and labelled inside the containment before anything crosses the barrier. Nothing gets carried through your house in an open container."
    - title: Air monitoring
      description: "Air is sampled while the work is happening, not only at the end. That is what tells us the containment is doing its job while there is still time to correct it, rather than discovering a problem at clearance when the work is already finished."
    - title: Decontamination
      description: "The work area, the equipment, and the crew are decontaminated before the containment comes down. HEPA vacuuming, wet wiping, and a final visual inspection of every surface inside the enclosure."
    - title: Disposal
      description: "Bagged material goes to a facility licensed to receive it, with the paperwork that proves it got there. Asbestos waste is not something that quietly goes into a dumpster, and the disposal record is part of what you are handed at the end."
    - title: Third-party clearance testing
      description: "An independent accredited laboratory performs the final clearance and issues the result directly. We do not collect those samples, we do not process them, and we do not interpret them. If the result is not clean, we go back in. This is the step the rest of the job exists to pass, and it is the one we deliberately do not control."

containment:
  heading: Containment, decon, and monitoring while the work runs
  paragraphs:
    - "A negative-pressure containment goes up before any material is disturbed. Every job, regardless of size. Two pre-filters plus HEPA, ducted to an exterior opening, pulling a vacuum so nothing airborne leaves the work area."
    - "On asbestos work the containment carries two things the mold version does not. A decontamination chamber at the entry, so the crew and their equipment cannot carry fibres out on the way through. And air monitoring while the removal is happening, so the enclosure is being verified during the work rather than assumed to be holding."
    - "The pressure inside the enclosure is lower than the pressure in the rest of the house. Air moves in and never out. That is the entire mechanism, and it is why we do not treat containment as something that scales down for a small job."

faqs:
  - question: Do I have to test before a remodel?
    answer: "If the house predates 1980 and the work will disturb ceilings, flooring, insulation, or ducting, testing first is the cheaper order of operations. A few samples and a lab result cost far less than stopping a demolition halfway through and abating a space that contractors and their equipment have already been working in. If you are planning work, call before the crew is booked rather than after."
  - question: Can I scrape a popcorn ceiling myself?
    answer: "Not until it has been tested, and often not afterwards either. Scraping is one of the most effective ways to put fibres into the air, and it happens in the room where you live rather than inside a containment. If the material tests negative, scrape away. If it tests positive, it is a contained job with clearance testing at the end, and doing it any other way leaves the house dirtier than it started."
  - question: What is vermiculite and why does it come up?
    answer: "Vermiculite is a loose-fill attic insulation that looks like small grey-brown pebbles. A large share of what was sold in this country came from a mine whose deposit was contaminated with asbestos, so it is treated as suspect until testing says otherwise. If you have it, the practical advice is to stay out of the attic and not disturb it while you decide what to do."
  - question: How do you know the material is actually gone?
    answer: "An independent accredited laboratory performs the final clearance testing and issues the result. We do not collect those samples, we do not run them, and we do not interpret them. Most abatement companies clear their own work, which means the company that wants to be paid is also the company saying the job passed. Ours is the one step in the process we deliberately do not control."
  - question: Does asbestos always have to be removed?
    answer: "No, and anyone telling you otherwise is selling. Intact, undamaged material that is not going to be disturbed can often be left in place and documented, or encapsulated so it cannot release fibres. Removal is the right answer when the material is damaged, when it is in the way of work you are doing, or when a sale or a lender requires it — the situation covered in [what to do when something is flagged on an inspection report](/mold-found-during-home-inspection/). The inspection tells you which situation you are in, and it is free."
  - question: What does asbestos abatement cost?
    answer: "We do not publish prices, because the number depends on the material, the square footage, how accessible it is, how complex the containment has to be, and whether reconstruction follows. Lab work and clearance testing are quoted separately from the removal. The inspection is free and ends with a written number rather than a range."
