  paragraphs:
    - "We do kitchens, bathrooms, basements, and whole-home remodels across the Treasure Valley. The work itself is ordinary remodeling: design decisions, demolition, framing, mechanical, drywall, finishes, a punch list."

This company spends most of its time removing mold, abating asbestos, and drying out buildings, which means we have spent years being called into other people's remodels after something went wrong inside a wall. That experience is the reason to hire us for the remodel itself rather than for the cleanup afterward."
    - "In a pre-1980 house that difference is concrete rather than philosophical. Textured ceilings, floor tile and the adhesive beneath it, duct and pipe wrap, attic insulation — all of them are candidates for testing before a demolition crew touches them, and a remodeler who treats that as somebody else's problem is handing you a schedule risk without telling you."
    - "On a newer house none of that applies and we will say so on the first visit. The materials question takes ten minutes to close out and then the job is a normal remodel."

problem:
  heading: What goes wrong when the walls are a surprise
  paragraphs:
    - "The version we get called into looks like this. Demolition is underway, a ceiling or a floor comes up, and it occurs to somebody that the material might contain asbestos. Everything stops. The space has already been worked in, so it now needs containing and cleaning rather than simply having the material removed, and the trades booked behind demolition are standing down."
    - "The second version is water. A wall opens and there is staining on the framing from a leak that has been running quietly for years. That is not a remodeling problem, it is a moisture problem that has to be solved before anything is closed back up, and a remodeler without that skill set either subcontracts it under time pressure or builds over it."
    - "Building over it is the outcome nobody discusses. New drywall goes onto framing that is still wet, the finish looks perfect at handover, and the problem re-emerges inside the first year — by which point it is inside your warranty and inside your new kitchen."
    - "Neither of those is exotic. In a valley where a large share of the housing predates 1980 and where crawlspace moisture is routine, they are the two most predictable ways a remodel goes sideways."

process:
  heading: How a remodel runs with us
  intro: "Seven steps. The second one is the one other remodelers tend not to have."
  steps:
    - title: Consultation and scope
      description: "We come out, look at what you want to do, and talk through what is realistic in the building you actually have. Older houses constrain layouts in ways that only show up once somebody has opened a few of them. This visit is free and it ends with a written scope rather than a verbal range."
    - title: Settling what is behind the wall
      description: "On a pre-1980 property we identify which materials your scope will disturb and tell you which are worth testing. An independent company does the sampling; you engage and pay them directly. We do not perform testing or take a percentage of it, so our advice about whether you need it costs us nothing to give. On a newer house this step usually closes out on the spot."
    - title: Abatement or remediation, if the result calls for it
      description: "Where testing comes back positive, or where the walls turn up mold or long-running moisture, that work happens first and it happens properly. It is the same crew, so there is no seam between the remediation and the remodel for a problem to fall through."
    - title: Selective demolition
      description: "What comes out, comes out. What stays is protected, and HVAC openings are sealed so debris does not travel into the duct system and out to rooms that are not part of the job. [Demolition](/demolition/) covers that work in its own right."
    - title: Structural, mechanical, and rough-in
      description: "Framing changes, plumbing, electrical, and HVAC as the scope requires, inspected where inspection applies. In an older house this is where surprises surface, and you hear about them with photographs before anything proceeds rather than at handover."
    - title: Drywall and finishes
      description: "Insulation, drywall, texture matched to the existing surfaces, paint, trim, flooring, cabinetry, and fixtures. Matching an old hand-applied texture is often the slowest part of a remodel in a pre-1980 house and it is where the work shows."
    - title: Walkthrough and the file
      description: "We go through the finished work with you and hand over the documentation: the scope, photographs of what was behind the walls, and where abatement or remediation happened, the clearance results that went with it. That file matters when you sell."

containment:
  heading: When a remodel turns into an abatement
  paragraphs:
    - "If testing comes back positive, the affected material is removed inside a negative-pressure containment before the remodel continues. Two pre-filters plus HEPA, ducted to an exterior opening, with a decontamination chamber at the entry and air monitored while the work runs."
    - "The scheduling consequence is the part worth knowing in advance: that containment is an exclusive work area. Nobody else works in it, so it takes its own slot in the program rather than overlapping with another trade. Planning for it is a few days; discovering it mid-demolition costs considerably more."
    - "Clearance testing at the end is carried out by an independent company we engage, and they issue the result. [What a proper containment looks like](/our-containment-process/) covers what will be standing in your house while the remodel is paused around it."

faqs:
  - question: Is this the same as your reconstruction service?
    answer: "No, and it is worth being clear about the difference. [Reconstruction](/reconstruction/) is rebuilding after a loss — you had water damage or a remediation, material came out, and it has to go back. It is usually insurance-involved and you did not choose to be doing it. A remodel is work you decided to do because you want a different kitchen. Same crew, same standards, entirely different starting point."
  - question: Do you work to an architect's drawings?
    answer: "Yes. We build to your designer's or architect's plans, and we will tell you early where an older building is going to argue with them — a bearing wall, a duct run, a floor that is not level in the way the drawing assumes. What we do not do is design-only work."
  - question: Do I need asbestos testing for a remodel?
    answer: "If the house predates 1980 and the work will disturb ceilings, flooring, insulation, or ducting, then yes, and doing it first is far cheaper than doing it after. Only the materials your project actually touches need sampling, which usually means fewer samples than owners expect. On a newer house it is generally unnecessary and we will say so."
  - question: What happens if you find mold once the walls are open?
    answer: "You get told, with photographs, before anything proceeds. Then it gets remediated properly — containment, removal, drying, and the moisture source corrected — before the remodel continues. It is the same crew, so there is no second contractor to schedule and no seam for a problem to fall through."
  - question: Can I live in the house during the work?
    answer: "For a single-room remodel, usually. Whole-home work is scheduled in phases so you are not without a kitchen and a bathroom at once. Where abatement is part of the job, that area is sealed and held under negative pressure while it runs, so the rest of the house stays usable."
  - question: What does a remodel cost?
    answer: "Every job has its own scope, its own complexity, and its own method, so one price list would be wrong more often than it was right. That is why the inspection is free and the estimate is written after someone has actually seen what you are dealing with. On a remodel the finish selections move the number as much as the construction does, which is why the scope is written after that conversation rather than before it."
