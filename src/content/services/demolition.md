problem:
  heading: What a stop-work actually costs
  paragraphs:
    - "The expensive version runs like this. Demolition starts on schedule. Partway through, someone lifts flooring or opens a ceiling and it occurs to somebody that the material might contain asbestos. Everything stops."
    - "Now the situation is worse than it would have been. The material has been disturbed while people were working in it, so the space needs containing and cleaning rather than simply having the material removed. Tools and materials in the room may need dealing with. Clearance testing is required on an area that would not have needed any. And the trade booked to follow demolition is standing down or gone."
    - "The quiet version is worse again in the long run. Nobody tests, nobody stops, the material goes into a skip with the rest of the demolition waste, and whatever settled stays in the building. There is no dramatic moment in that version, which is exactly why it keeps happening."
    - "The cost of avoiding both is a few samples and about a week of lead time. [Sequencing asbestos testing around a remodel](/asbestos-testing-before-remodel/) covers the calendar in detail, and it is written to be forwarded to whoever is holding the schedule."

process:
  heading: How a demolition job runs
  intro: "The order changes depending on the age of the building. Steps one and two are what a demolition contractor without a remediation background will usually skip."
  steps:
    - title: Walkthrough and material assessment
      description: "Free, and it happens before anything is quoted. We look at what your scope will actually disturb and identify which materials are suspect. On a pre-1980 building that list is what determines whether testing is needed and how much. On a newer one it usually takes ten minutes and produces nothing."
    - title: Testing, where the age of the building calls for it
      description: "An independent testing company samples the suspect materials; you engage them and pay them directly. We do not perform testing or take a percentage of it. Nothing gets demolished on an assumption in either direction — assuming material is clean is how buildings get contaminated, and assuming it is not is how owners pay for abatement they never needed."
    - title: Abatement first, if a result comes back positive
      description: "Asbestos-containing material comes out as its own job, inside its own containment with air monitoring, and an independent company clears the space before general demolition begins. [Asbestos abatement](/asbestos-abatement/) covers that sequence in full."
    - title: Protection and containment of what stays
      description: "On a selective or interior job, the parts of the building that are not coming out get protected. Sheeting, floor protection, and sealed HVAC openings so dust does not travel through the duct system into rooms that are not part of the work."
    - title: Utilities and disconnections
      description: "Water, power, and gas serving the demolition area are isolated before anything is opened. Cutting a live line is the most common avoidable incident on a strip-out and it is entirely a sequencing problem."
    - title: The demolition itself
      description: "Interior strip-out, selective removal, or full takedown depending on the scope. Selective work is slower per square foot than it looks, because the difficulty is in what has to survive rather than what comes out."
    - title: Haul-off and disposal
      description: "Material is sorted where sorting is worthwhile, loaded, and taken to the appropriate facility with the documentation to show where it went. Asbestos-containing waste goes to a facility licensed to receive it and carries its own record."

faqs:
  - question: Do I need asbestos testing before demolition?
    answer: "If the building predates 1980 and the work will disturb ceilings, flooring, insulation, or ducting, then yes, and doing it first is far cheaper than doing it after. Only materials your scope actually disturbs need sampling, which usually means fewer samples than owners expect. On a newer building it is generally unnecessary and we will say so."
  - question: Can you do the demolition and the abatement?
    answer: "Yes, and where a result comes back positive that is usually the sensible arrangement — one scope, one crew, one point of contact, and no seam between two contractors for a problem to fall through. What we will not do is produce the test result that decides whether abatement is needed."
  - question: What is selective demolition?
    answer: "Taking out specific elements while the rest of the structure stays intact and undamaged — a wall, a kitchen, a bathroom, a single floor of a building. It is slower per square foot than a full strip-out because the work is in protecting what survives rather than in removing what does not."
  - question: Do you do full teardowns?
    answer: "Yes, and the same sequencing applies. A pre-1980 structure coming down entirely still needs its materials established first, and the disposal documentation matters more rather than less, because there is no building left afterward to inspect."
  - question: What happens to the debris?
    answer: "It is hauled to the appropriate facility and you get documentation showing where it went. Asbestos-containing waste goes to a facility licensed to receive it and carries its own record, which is part of the file if the property is ever sold or inspected."
  - question: What does demolition cost?
    answer: "Every job has its own scope, its own complexity, and its own method, so one price list would be wrong more often than it was right. That is why the inspection is free and the estimate is written after someone has actually seen what you are dealing with. On demolition the biggest variable is usually how much has to survive, not how much comes out."
