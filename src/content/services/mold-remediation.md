intro:
  heading: What mold remediation actually is
  paragraphs:
    - "Remediation is not cleaning. Cleaning moves mold around. Remediation removes the material the mold is growing on, filters the air while that happens, and corrects the water that caused it. Leave any one of those three out and you are booking the same job again in six months."
    - "Mold needs moisture, something organic to grow on, and time. In a Treasure Valley house that usually means a crawlspace holding water, a supply line that let go behind a wall, a water heater that failed slowly enough that nobody noticed, or a bathroom fan that has been venting into a joist bay for years. The mold is the visible part. The water is the actual problem, and it is the part that decides whether the work holds."
    - "Federal guidance on mold comes down to two things: remove it, and fix the moisture that let it grow. That is the whole job. Everything below is how we do those two things without contaminating the rest of your house on the way."
    - "Calls come from three directions, mostly. A homeowner who found something dark behind a baseboard or smelled something coming up from the crawlspace. A buyer or seller whose home inspector flagged growth and who now has a closing date to protect. A landlord or property manager who needs a unit turned and documented. Different pressure, same work."

problem:
  heading: What goes wrong when it is handled badly
  paragraphs:
    - "Mold sitting undisturbed on a joist is a problem. Mold that gets scrubbed, scraped, or torn out with no containment is a bigger one. Disturbing growth is what puts spores in the air, and air moves through a house faster than most people expect: through open doors, through return ducts, through the gaps around plumbing penetrations."
    - "Bleach is the most common mistake. On a hard, non-porous surface it has a role. On framing lumber, drywall, or subfloor, which are porous, it mostly adds water to material that already has a moisture problem. The surface looks clean. The growth continues underneath it."
    - "Painting over growth is the same mistake with better optics. So is fogging a crawlspace and calling it finished. Both leave the affected material in place, and neither one touches the water."
    - "Then there is the failure that costs a closing rather than a callback. A remediation gets done, nothing is documented, and no independent party ever verifies it. Weeks later a buyer's agent asks for proof the work was completed and cleared. There is a receipt and nothing else. That is a hard position to argue from, and it is avoidable with paperwork that should have existed from day one."

process:
  heading: How a mold remediation job runs
  intro: "Seven steps, in this order, on every job. The order is not negotiable. Containment before removal is the entire point."
  steps:
    - title: Inspection and assessment
      description: "Someone comes out and looks at it. We map how far the growth actually extends, take moisture readings through the surrounding material, and work backward to the water source. You get a written estimate at the end of it. This visit is free."
    - title: Containment
      description: "Poly sheeting goes up around the work area and every seam gets taped. A negative air machine runs two pre-filters into a HEPA final stage and ducts to an exterior opening. Where the layout calls for it, we build a decontamination chamber at the entry so nothing walks out on a boot."
    - title: HEPA air filtration
      description: "Air scrubbers run inside the containment for the duration of the job, not just during demolition. They exchange the air in the work area continuously, which is what keeps the airborne count falling while material is being disturbed."
    - title: Removal
      description: "Affected porous material comes out. Drywall gets cut back past the visible growth line, wet insulation is bagged, and contaminated finish material is removed rather than treated in place. Everything is bagged inside the containment before it crosses the barrier."
    - title: Cleaning and sanitizing
      description: "What stays gets HEPA vacuumed, wiped down, and treated with an antimicrobial. This is the framing, the subfloor, and the structure you are keeping. Done properly it is slow, and it is the step most often rushed."
    - title: Restoration
      description: "We rebuild what we took out. Drywall, texture, paint, insulation, vapor barrier. Same company, same crew, one point of contact, which means nobody is standing in your living room explaining that the rebuild is somebody else's scope."
    - title: Final inspection
      description: "We verify the structure reads dry and the work area is visibly clean. Where the job calls for it, an independent lab performs clearance testing and issues the result. We do not run those samples ourselves."

faqs:
  - question: Do I need to move out during mold remediation?
    answer: "Usually not, and the containment is the reason. The work area is sealed off and held under negative pressure, so the rest of the house stays usable while we work. The exceptions are jobs that take out your only bathroom, jobs inside the HVAC system, and jobs large enough that the containment swallows most of the floor plan. You will know which one you have at the estimate, not on day two."
  - question: Can I just clean it with bleach?
    answer: "Sometimes, and we will tell you when. Small growth on a hard, non-porous surface that is already dry may only need cleaning. On porous material like drywall, framing, or subfloor, bleach wets material that already has a moisture problem and leaves the growth below the surface. If your situation is the first kind, we would rather say so at the free inspection than sell you a containment you do not need."
  - question: How do you know the mold is actually gone?
    answer: "Two ways. We verify visually and with moisture readings that the structure is dry and clean. Then, where the job calls for it, an independent accredited lab performs clearance testing. We do not collect and process those samples ourselves and we do not interpret them in our own favor. The company doing the work should not be the company certifying the work is done."
  - question: Will homeowners insurance cover mold remediation?
    answer: "It depends on where the water came from. Sudden and accidental damage, such as a supply line that burst, is commonly covered. Long-term seepage and deferred maintenance commonly are not. Your policy language decides it, not us. What we control is the documentation: we photograph conditions before anything is touched, record moisture as it changes, and write a scope specific enough to be evaluated on facts rather than argued over. The distinction insurers draw is [sudden damage versus long-term seepage](/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-mold/), and it is usually decided by where the water came from rather than how bad the growth looks."
  - question: What does mold remediation cost in Idaho?
    answer: "We do not publish a price, because the number is driven by things nobody can know before looking at it. Square footage, whether the growth is in a crawlspace or in finished living space, whether drywall has to come out, how complex the containment has to be, and whether reconstruction follows all move it. The inspection is free and it ends with a written number rather than a range, which is more useful to you than [a published price range that fits nobody's house](/mold-remediation-cost-idaho/)."
  - question: What if the mold is in my crawl space?
    answer: "That is a large share of what we do here. Treasure Valley crawlspaces contend with a high water table, irrigation runoff, grading that sends water toward the house, and vapor barriers that have torn or gone missing. The remediation sequence is the same, but the durable fix usually includes [crawlspace drainage](/crawlspace-drainage/) or [encapsulation](/crawlspace-encapsulation/). Without one of those, you are scheduling the same job again after the next wet spring."
