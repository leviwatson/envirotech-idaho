intro:
  heading: The cheapest day to test is before the demolition
  paragraphs:
    - "Most people asking about asbestos testing are not reacting to a problem. They are planning a kitchen, a bathroom, new flooring, or a ceiling scrape, the house was built before 1980, and somebody sensible told them to check first."
    - "That is the right instinct and the right order of operations. A few bulk samples and a laboratory result add a handful of days to the schedule and cost a small fraction of an abatement. What they buy you is not discovering the problem partway through demolition, with material already disturbed, a crew standing down, and a space that now has to be contained and cleaned before anyone can go back in."
    - "Two companies are involved and it is worth knowing which is which. The testing is done by an independent testing company — they collect the samples, their laboratory analyses them, and they issue the result to you. You engage them and you pay them. We do not perform that work or take a percentage of it."
    - "We are the abatement contractor. Our inspection is free, and it is useful before you commission anything: we can tell you which materials your project will actually disturb, which is what determines how much testing you need. Plenty of houses need far fewer samples than an owner expects."

problem:
  heading: What happens when the order gets reversed
  paragraphs:
    - "The expensive version goes like this. Demolition starts, someone pulls up flooring or opens a ceiling, and partway through it occurs to somebody that the material might contain asbestos. Work stops. The question is no longer whether to test, it is what to do about a space that has already been disturbed while people were in it."
    - "That is a containment, a cleaning, air monitoring, and clearance testing on a room that would have needed none of it if samples had gone to a laboratory a week earlier. It is also a contractor standing down who is booked elsewhere next week."
    - "The quieter version is worse. Nobody tests, nobody stops, the material goes into a skip with the rest of the demolition waste, and the household lives with whatever settled. There is no dramatic moment in that version, which is exactly why it keeps happening."
    - "In pre-1980 Treasure Valley housing the usual suspects are textured ceilings, 9x9 floor tile and the black mastic underneath, duct and pipe wrap in crawlspaces, vermiculite in attics, and some siding and roofing material. None of it is identifiable by eye with any confidence, which is the whole reason laboratories exist."

process:
  heading: How testing runs, and who does which part
  intro: "Step one is ours and it is free. From step two onward you are dealing with the testing company."
  steps:
    - title: Free inspection
      description: "We come out and identify which materials your project will actually disturb. Not everything in an old house needs testing — what matters is what the work touches. This narrows the sampling before you have paid anyone for it, and it is the cheapest hour in the whole sequence."
    - title: You engage a testing company
      description: "You contract an independent testing company directly and pay them directly. We are not in that transaction and we take nothing from it. If you want names of firms working in the valley, ask and we will give you some, with no arrangement behind it."
    - title: Sample collection
      description: "Their technician takes samples wet wherever wetting is appropriate and keeps them small, because collecting a sample should not create the problem you are testing for. Materials are sampled in groups, since a result only speaks for the material it came from — a ceiling and a floor are two separate questions."
    - title: Chain of custody
      description: "Each sample is bagged, labeled, and logged against a form recording who handled it and when. That form is what makes the result usable by an insurer, a buyer, or a contractor deciding whether it is safe to start."
    - title: Laboratory analysis
      description: "The laboratory determines whether asbestos is present and at what concentration, and the testing company issues the report to you. You commissioned it, so it is yours, and it does not commit you to anybody."
    - title: Reading the result
      description: "Bring it to us and we will go through it in plain language at no cost. A positive on one material and a negative on another is common and useful — it usually means a smaller job than anyone expected."
    - title: What happens next
      description: "A result is a decision, not a verdict. Depending on what came back and what you are doing, the answer is removal, encapsulation, or leaving intact material alone and documenting it. If it is removal, [asbestos abatement](/asbestos-abatement/) covers the sequence, and the clearance testing at the end is carried out by an independent company we engage rather than by us."

containment:
  heading: If a sample comes back positive
  paragraphs:
    - "A positive result is not an emergency and it does not mean the house has to be emptied. It means the material cannot be disturbed by ordinary demolition, and the work needs doing inside a containment by people set up for it."
    - "That containment goes up before any material is disturbed. Two pre-filters plus HEPA, ducted to an exterior opening, pulling a vacuum so nothing airborne leaves the work area, with a decontamination chamber at the entry so nothing walks out on a boot. Air is monitored during removal rather than only at the end."
    - "The final clearance is carried out by an independent company we engage, and they issue the result. Most abatement firms clear their own work. That is legal, it is common, and it puts the company that wants to be paid in charge of saying the job passed."

faqs:
  - question: Do you do the asbestos testing yourselves?
    answer: "No. An independent testing company collects the samples, their laboratory analyses them, and they issue the result to you. You engage them and pay them directly. We are the abatement contractor — the company that would be paid to remove whatever is found — which is exactly why we should not be the company producing the result."
  - question: Do I need to test before a remodel?
    answer: "If the building predates 1980 and the work will disturb ceilings, flooring, insulation, or ducting, testing first is the cheaper order of operations by a wide margin. Only materials your project actually touches need sampling, and our free inspection will tell you which those are before you commission anything."
  - question: How many samples will I need?
    answer: "It depends how many distinct materials are in scope, because a result only speaks for the material it came from. The testing company sets the sample plan and quotes it. What we can do beforehand, for free, is tell you which materials your project will disturb, which is usually fewer than an owner assumes."
  - question: What does a positive result actually mean?
    answer: "That the material contains asbestos at the concentration stated. It does not mean the house is unsafe to occupy or that everything must come out. Intact material that is not being disturbed can often stay in place and be documented, or be encapsulated. Removal is the answer when it is damaged, when it is in the way of your work, or when a sale or lender requires it."
  - question: How long do results take?
    answer: "Two to five days is typical for standard analysis, with rush processing sometimes available at extra cost. That timeline belongs to the testing company, so confirm it with them. If a contractor is already booked, raise it early — sequencing around a laboratory is much easier a week out than the morning demolition was meant to start."
  - question: What about mold testing?
    answer: "Same arrangement, different sampling. [How mold testing works](/mold-inspection-testing/) covers bulk, surface, and air sampling and when each is worth commissioning. In both cases the inspection is ours and free, the testing belongs to an independent company you engage, and the result is yours."
