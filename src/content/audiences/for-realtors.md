faqs:
  - question: How fast can you get to a property?
    answer: "One to two days for non-emergency work, and a written estimate usually the same day as the visit. Active water damage gets a response any hour, because the damage compounds while it sits. If a closing date is driving it, say so on the first call and it will be factored into the schedule."
  - question: Will the work hold up to the buyer's inspection?
    answer: "That is what the clearance report is for. An independent accredited lab performs the final testing and issues the result, so what your client hands over is a third-party document rather than our assurance. If the buyer wants their own test on top of it, we would rather they did."
  - question: Can a house still be shown while work is happening?
    answer: "Usually, yes. The work area is sealed off and held under negative pressure, so the rest of the property stays usable. The exceptions are jobs that take out the only bathroom, work inside the HVAC system, and jobs large enough that the containment covers most of the floor plan. You will know which one you have at the estimate."
  - question: Do you work with the adjuster if there is a claim?
    answer: "Yes. We bill the insurer directly and work with the adjuster, and we document the scope so it holds up. Sudden and accidental damage such as a failed supply line is commonly covered; long-term seepage and deferred maintenance commonly are not. The policy decides that, not us."
  - question: What does it cost?
    answer: "We do not publish prices, because the number depends on square footage, whether the growth is in a crawlspace or in finished space, whether drywall comes out, and whether reconstruction follows. The inspection is free and ends with a written number rather than a range, which is what makes the negotiation short."
  - question: Do you handle asbestos as well as mold?
    answer: "Yes, and it comes up often in pre-1980 Treasure Valley housing — popcorn ceilings, floor tile, vermiculite insulation, duct wrap. Asbestos runs its own sequence with air monitoring throughout and third-party clearance at the end. If a remodel is planned after closing, testing before demolition is worth raising with your client early."
