    paragraphs:
      - "Everything about working with us on an older property comes down to one thing: when does the material get tested. Test a week ahead and a positive result is a scheduled task inside your program. Test after demolition starts and it is a stop-work, a contained clean of a space your crew has already been working in, and a start date you now have to renegotiate with an owner."

faqs:
  - question: How much lead time do you need before demolition?
    answer: "A week is comfortable, two if the scope is large or several materials are involved. That covers our free inspection, the owner engaging a testing company, sampling, and laboratory turnaround, with enough margin that a positive result can be abated without moving your start date. The morning demolition was meant to begin is far too late, and that is when a lot of these calls arrive."
  - question: Can abatement run alongside another trade in the same area?
    answer: "No. The containment is an exclusive work area while it is running, and nobody works in it until an independent company has cleared the space. It needs its own slot in the program. Planning for overlap is the most common way these jobs cost a GC days they did not budget."
  - question: Who engages and pays the testing company?
    answer: "The owner does, directly. We do not perform testing, sell it, or take a percentage, so it never appears in our number and never sits inside your contract. Our inspection beforehand is free, including telling you which materials are worth sampling and which are not."
  - question: What if we find something mid-demolition?
    answer: "Stop and call. What matters then is whether the space needs containing and cleaning before anyone continues, which depends on what was disturbed and how much. It is a worse position than testing first and it is far better than continuing. We would rather take that call at nine in the morning than see the job again in a month."
  - question: What does abatement cost on a job like this?
    answer: "Every job has its own scope, its own complexity, and its own method, so one price list would be wrong more often than it was right. That is why the inspection is free and the estimate is written after someone has actually seen what you are dealing with. For a GC that estimate is usually the document that lets the owner make a decision quickly."
