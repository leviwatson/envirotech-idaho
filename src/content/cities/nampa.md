housingStock:
  heading: The water arrives on a schedule
  paragraphs:
    - "Nampa sits on irrigated agricultural ground in Canyon County, and the water table here moves with the season. When the canals run, it comes up. When they stop, it drops. A great many Nampa houses have a crawlspace that is wet for part of the year and dry for the rest, and the owners have often known about it for a decade without treating it as a problem."
    - "That seasonality is what makes Nampa different from anywhere else in the valley. In Boise a wet crawlspace usually means something failed. In Nampa it frequently means nothing failed at all — the house was built with a shallow crawlspace and a dirt floor on ground that takes on water every year, and it is doing exactly what that arrangement produces."
    - "The consequence is a moisture load running under the floor for months at a time. Joists and subfloor take it on continuously, insulation gets wet and falls out of the bays, and a house moves crawlspace air upward into living space whether anyone wants it to or not. The smell people notice in a Nampa hallway in June is usually crawlspace air arriving upstairs."
    - "Because the water is seasonal, so is the discovery. A crawlspace inspected in September can look entirely fine and be underwater in May. That is worth knowing before buying a house here, and it is worth knowing before accepting that a previous remediation was successful."

localDetail:
  heading: Why remediation alone does not hold here
  paragraphs:
    - "This is the part worth being blunt about. On a Nampa crawlspace with seasonal water, removing the growth and cleaning the space is not a solution. It is a pause. The canals run again the following spring, the water comes back to the same place, and the same joists grow the same thing."
    - "We have been called to crawlspaces that were remediated properly by somebody else and failed anyway, because the scope stopped at the mold and never touched the water. That work was not incompetent, it was incomplete, and the homeowner paid twice for the same problem."
    - "So a Nampa job usually has drainage in front of it or alongside it. Finding whether the water is groundwater, surface run-off, or a plumbing failure is the first question of the inspection, because those three have completely different fixes and they look identical under a flashlight."
    - "Where groundwater is the driver, the order is drainage first, then remediation, then a vapor barrier or encapsulation over ground that is no longer taking on water. Sealing a wet Nampa crawlspace without drainage traps the water inside the envelope, which is worse than leaving it alone."

faqs:
  - question: Everyone here has water under the house. Is it actually a problem?
    answer: "Common is not the same as harmless. A crawlspace holding water through irrigation season is putting moisture into the framing above it continuously, and that air moves up into living space rather than staying under the floor. Plenty of Nampa houses have lived with it for years, and the cost of that shows up as rot at the sill and repeated growth rather than as a dramatic event."
  - question: Do I need drainage or just remediation?
    answer: "If the water is seasonal groundwater, you need both, and drainage first. Removing growth from a crawlspace that fills again in spring buys you until spring. The inspection separates groundwater from surface water from a plumbing leak, because those look the same under a flashlight and have entirely different fixes."
  - question: My crawlspace was cleaned two years ago and it is back. Why?
    answer: "Almost always because the scope stopped at the mold. If the water source was never addressed, the conditions that produced the growth are unchanged and the growth returns to the same joists. That is not a failure of the cleaning; it is a scope that was incomplete."

    answer: "Yes, and it is worth telling us what you have seen in spring. Moisture meters and staining tell us a good deal about what the crawlspace does at its wettest even when it is dry on the day, but your account of what it looks like in May is genuinely useful information."
