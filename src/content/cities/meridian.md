housingStock:
  heading: New houses, predictable failures
  paragraphs:
    - "Almost all of Meridian was built during the growth of the last three decades, which changes the mold conversation completely. There is no asbestos question, the plumbing is modern, and the crawlspaces are generally dry. What you get instead is a set of failures that come from how quickly and how uniformly the housing was built."
    - "The single most common one we find here is a bathroom exhaust fan that does not vent outside. It terminates in the attic, or into a joist bay, or into a soffit that pushes the air straight back under the roof. The fan runs, the homeowner assumes the moisture is leaving, and it is depositing into insulation and sheathing a few feet above the ceiling instead."
    - "That failure is invisible for years. It shows up as staining on a bedroom ceiling, or a smell in a closet that shares a wall with the bathroom, long after the sheathing above has been taking on moisture every shower. By the time anyone looks, the affected area is usually larger than the stain suggests."
    - "The second Meridian pattern is water arriving from above rather than below. Second-floor laundry, refrigerator supply lines to icemakers, upstairs bathrooms over finished living space. When any of those fail, the water travels down through the floor structure and the damage runs across two storeys rather than one room."

  heading: The failures that repeat here
  intro: "Tract-built housing means the same house was built many times, so the same failure appears in the same place across a street."
  items:
    - name: Bath fans venting into the attic
      detail: "The most common single finding in Meridian. The duct is connected at the fan and terminates somewhere that is not outside. Everything looks correct from inside the bathroom, which is why it goes unnoticed until the ceiling below stains."
    - name: Second-floor laundry
      detail: "A supply line or a drain pan failure on an upper floor puts water into a ceiling cavity and down interior walls. The visible damage is often on the floor below, some distance from the appliance, which sends people looking in the wrong place."
    - name: Refrigerator and icemaker lines
      detail: "A slow weep behind a fridge runs under cabinetry and into the subfloor with nothing visible on the surface. It is a common cause of kitchen subfloor mold in otherwise dry, modern houses."
    - name: Attic condensation
      detail: "Insufficient ventilation combined with warm moist air from the living space produces condensation on the underside of the roof deck in cold weather. It reads as a roof leak and is not one, and fixing the roof does not fix it."
    - name: Grading and downspouts
      detail: "Where a Meridian crawlspace is wet, the cause is usually surface water rather than the water table — soil sloping back toward the house, or downspouts discharging at the foundation."

    answer: "In Meridian that is very often a fan that vents into the attic or a joist bay rather than outside.   - question: My house is only fifteen years old. Can it really have mold?
    answer: "Yes, and age is not much protection. Mold needs moisture, and a modern house has plenty of ways to deliver it — a fan terminating in the wrong place, a supply line to an icemaker, an upstairs laundry. What newer construction does give you is fewer complications during removal, because there is rarely a hazardous material question in the way."
  - question: Is a wet crawlspace in Meridian the same problem as in Nampa?
