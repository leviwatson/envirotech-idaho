housingStock:
  heading: Why Boise jobs so often become two jobs
  paragraphs:
    - "Boise has the oldest concentrated housing stock in the valley. The North End, the Bench, and the older parts of the East End are substantially pre-1980, and a great deal of it is pre-1960. That single fact drives almost everything about how mold work runs here."
    - "In a house built before 1980, the material around the mold is as much of a question as the mold itself. Textured ceilings, 9x9 floor tile with black mastic under it, duct and pipe wrap in the crawlspace, vermiculite in an attic that was insulated before anyone thought twice about it. None of that is a problem sitting still. All of it becomes a problem the moment somebody cuts into it to reach wet framing."
    - "So a Boise remediation frequently has a step in front of it that a newer house does not: finding out what else is in the wall before opening it. That is not us being cautious for the sake of it. Removing drywall in a 1948 bungalow without knowing what the ceiling texture contains is how a contained mold job turns into an uncontained asbestos job, and the second one is considerably more expensive to undo."
    - "The other Boise-specific factor is basements. They are far more common here than in Nampa or Caldwell, and a basement changes the problem — below-grade walls, slower drying, and finished basement space that puts carpet and drywall against a foundation that was never detailed to stay dry."

localDetail:
  heading: What that means for the order of the work
  paragraphs:
    - "On a pre-1980 Boise house we want to know what the suspect materials are before demolition rather than during it. That usually means a laboratory result on the ceiling texture or the flooring in the affected area, and the testing is done by an independent company you engage directly — not by us."
    - "If it comes back negative, the job is a straightforward mold remediation and the extra step cost you a few days. If it comes back positive, the sequence changes: the asbestos-containing material is abated first, inside a containment with air monitoring, with third-party clearance before the mold work continues into that space."
    - "That is the whole reason to test in the right order. A crew that opens a Bench ceiling to chase a roof leak, without knowing what the texture is, can spread material through a house in an afternoon and leave a problem that costs more than the original leak by a wide margin."
    - "Boise basements add a second wrinkle. Below-grade drying is slower than a crawlspace or a main floor, so a job that would run three days elsewhere sometimes runs four or five here, and most of that difference is equipment running rather than anyone working."

faqs:
  - question: My house is on the Bench and has a popcorn ceiling. Can you just remove the mold?
    answer: "Not until the ceiling has been tested. On pre-1980 Bench housing, textured ceilings are a common source of asbestos-containing material, and cutting into one to reach wet framing above is how a contained job becomes an uncontained one. An independent company tests it — you engage them directly — and the result decides the order of the work."
  - question: Do North End basements need different treatment?
    answer: "The sequence is the same but the timeline is usually longer. Below-grade walls dry more slowly than a crawlspace or a main floor, so the final stretch of machines running tends to add a day or two. Older North End houses also frequently have plaster rather than drywall, which behaves differently when wet and cannot simply be cut back to the next stud."
  - question: Is mold worse in older Boise homes?
    answer: "Not inherently — mold needs moisture regardless of the decade. What differs is what surrounds it. An older house is more likely to have materials that complicate the removal, and more likely to have original plumbing and drainage that produced the moisture in the first place."
  - question: How much does mold remediation cost in Boise?
    answer: "We do not publish prices, because the number depends on the area affected, whether it is a crawlspace or finished space, whether drywall or plaster has to come out, and whether asbestos-containing material is in the way. The inspection is free and ends with a written figure."
