housingStock:
  heading: Two building stocks, one address
  paragraphs:
    - "Eagle is mostly newer and larger construction on bigger lots, and on the house itself the problems look much like Meridian's: modern materials, modern plumbing, and failures that come from fixtures rather than from age. Longer plumbing runs and more finished square footage mean a failure travels further before anyone notices, but the mechanism is familiar."
    - "What makes Eagle distinct is what else is on the parcel. Acreage properties routinely carry a shop, a barn, a detached garage, a well house, or a guest structure, and those buildings are frequently decades older than the home in front of them. Some predate the subdivision of the land entirely."
    - "Those older structures are a different problem in every respect. They may have asbestos-containing material where the house has none. They often have no vapor barrier, minimal insulation, and drainage that was adequate for a barn and is not adequate now that the building is heated and used as a workshop or an office."
    - "They also get inspected far less often. A homeowner notices a stain on a bedroom ceiling within days. A slow leak in an outbuilding that is visited twice a month can run for a year, and by the time it is found the affected area is substantial."

    - "An older outbuilding also changes what has to happen before work starts. Adding heat and occupancy to a structure with bare ground underneath and no barrier is a reliable way to produce condensation that was never a problem when the building was cold and ventilated."

faqs:
  - question: Should you look at my shop as well as the house?
    answer: "Yes, and we would ask to. On Eagle acreage the outbuildings are frequently older than the home and have completely different problems, and the structure that prompted the call is not always the one in worse condition. There is no charge for looking — the inspection is free either way."
  - question: My barn is older than the house. Does that change anything?
    answer: "It can change the order of the work. A structure built decades before the house may contain asbestos-containing material where the house does not, and if so that has to be established by an independent testing company before anything is opened. On the house alone that step is often unnecessary."
  - question: Why does my outbuilding smell now that I have heated it?
    answer: "That is a common Eagle sequence. A cold, ventilated structure over bare ground behaves very differently once it is heated and occupied — warm moist air meets cold surfaces and condenses, and bare soil evaporates upward into a space that now holds that moisture. The fix usually involves a ground barrier as well as the removal."
  - question: Why did water from an upstairs bathroom show up in the basement?
    answer: "Larger homes give water more places to travel. It runs along joists and down wall cavities and emerges wherever it first meets something it cannot pass, which is often well away from the failure. That is why the inspection maps moisture rather than treating the stain as the boundary."
